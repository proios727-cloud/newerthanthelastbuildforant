## Every entry by failure mode

### Wears out early (14)

- **Vacuum brush rolls** (cleaning, opp. 48): Hair and thread wind tightly around the brush roll and its ends until it stops spinning, so it has to be cut free with scissors
- **Mattresses** (bedroom, opp. 31): Foam mattresses sag into body impressions within a few years, and warranty claims fail on high depth thresholds
- **Shower caddies** (bathroom, opp. 30): Suction cups let go on textured tile and the caddy crashes down; wire caddies sold as rust-resistant rust within months
- **Pillows** (bedroom, opp. 30): Fiberfill pillows come out of the wash lumpy and flat even when the care tag is followed, so many people stop washing them
- **Cordless stick vacuums** (cleaning, opp. 30): The battery is built in, runs far short of the box claim on max power, and when it wears out replacing the whole vacuum is cheaper
- **Basket-style air fryers** (kitchen, opp. 30): Mesh and crevices trap grease; the basket is hard to fully clean
- **Nonstick pans & air-fryer baskets** (kitchen, opp. 29): Coating flakes within weeks or months, sometimes after one wash; users worry about eating the flakes
- **Bathroom exhaust fans** (bathroom, opp. 23): Older fans get loud (often around 4 sones), and parts loosen and rattle with age, so people avoid running them
- **Dressers** (bedroom, opp. 23): Thin particle-board or cardboard drawer bottoms bow and fall out, and runners stick or let drawers drop onto each other
- **Mops & mop buckets** (cleaning, opp. 23): Wringers leave the mop dripping or don't fit the bucket, pads fray or catch, and buckets tip and spill dirty water
- **Food storage containers** (kitchen, opp. 23): Locking tabs snap off and lids stop sealing or don't fit
- **Shower curtain liners** (bathroom, opp. 21): Pink slime and mildew grow in the damp folds of the liner, which then has to be washed or thrown away
- **Toilet paper holders** (bathroom, opp. 19): Swapping the roll on a spring-loaded rod takes two hands, and the spring fails or the arm falls off
- **Resealable zipper bags** (packaging, opp. 15): The zipper tracks don't line up or come apart, and the easy-open tab tears off, so the bag never truly seals

### One size fits all (13)

- **Fitted sheets** (bedroom, opp. 48): Corners pop off overnight because pocket depths aren't standardized, the elastic is only at the corners, and toppers or adjustable bases add height or bend the mattress
- **Duvets & duvet covers** (bedroom, opp. 38): The insert slides and bunches inside the cover overnight, because nothing holds it in place and insert and cover sizes don't match
- **Child-resistant pill bottles** (packaging, opp. 30): Push-and-twist caps are painful or impossible for people with arthritis, leading to missed doses or bottles left open
- **Mattresses** (bedroom, opp. 23): Memory foam traps body heat and sleepers wake up sweaty, even on models marketed with cooling gel
- **Robot vacuums** (cleaning, opp. 23): The robot snags on socks, cords and rug tassels or wedges under low furniture, so owners tidy the floor before every run
- **Bedding in washers & dryers** (laundry, opp. 23): Fitted sheets and duvet covers wad into a damp ball that swallows socks and small items and comes out wet in the middle
- **Kitchen lighting** (kitchen, opp. 22): Ceiling lights sit behind the cook and throw shadows onto the worktop
- **Duvets & duvet covers** (bedroom, opp. 21): Getting the insert into the cover is a two-person job unless you know the 'burrito' trick, because both far corners have to be held at once
- **Shower curtain liners** (bathroom, opp. 19): The liner billows inward and sticks to the person showering
- **Cordless stick vacuums** (cleaning, opp. 19): The trigger has to be held down the whole time and can't be locked on, which makes hands sore during longer cleans
- **Low-flow toilets** (bathroom, opp. 15): Older low-flow models with narrow trapways clog and need double flushing (much less common with newer models)
- **Shampoo & conditioner bottles** (bathroom, opp. 14): The two bottles are nearly identical and labeled in tiny type, so without glasses in a dim shower people can't tell them apart
- **French-door refrigerators** (kitchen, opp. 11): Water and icemaker lines run through usable space, freeze, or take up shelf room

### Seller-first (8)

- **Plastic wrap / cling film** (kitchen, opp. 48): The serrated cutter loses the film's edge, the wrap clings to itself, and the blade cuts fingers
- **Online-order shipping boxes** (packaging, opp. 41): Tiny items arrive in huge boxes stuffed with filler, wasting cardboard and space for recycling
- **Chip bags & snack boxes** (packaging, opp. 41): Bags and boxes are half empty, so buyers feel they're paying for air, especially after shrinkflation cuts
- **Clamshell blister packs** (packaging, opp. 38): Sealed plastic clamshells need scissors or a knife to open, and opening them sends thousands of people a year to the emergency room with cuts
- **Liquid detergent caps** (laundry, opp. 26): Faint or confusing fill lines inside the cap lead people to use too much detergent, which wastes money and leaves residue on clothes and in the machine
- **Peel-off lids (yogurt, tins)** (kitchen, opp. 21): The tab tears off or the foil rips partway, and food splashes or the user gets cut
- **Snack & chip bags** (kitchen, opp. 21): Once opened, the bag can't be resealed and the food goes stale
- **Cereal box liners** (packaging, opp. 21): The inner bag rips unevenly down the side when opened, spills cereal into the box, and can't be reclosed

### No feedback (5)

- **Kitchen sponges** (cleaning, opp. 32): Sponges start to smell and harbor bacteria within days, yet most people keep them for a month or more because nothing shows when one is spent
- **Bathroom exhaust fans** (bathroom, opp. 30): The fan sounds like it's working but doesn't remove moisture (it's undersized, has a clogged grille, or is switched off too soon), which leads to mold and peeling paint
- **Clothes dryers** (laundry, opp. 29): Lint gets past the filter and builds up behind the drum or in the vent, where owners can't see or reach it, until it starts a fire
- **Recycling labels** (packaging, opp. 29): Recycling symbols are misread (the Green Dot is taken to mean recyclable), so many people guess or put packaging in the general waste
- **Clothing care labels** (laundry, opp. 23): More than three dozen care symbols that most people can't read, so clothes get shrunk or ruined in the wash

### Last-mile waste (3)

- **Front-load washers** (laundry, opp. 36): Water and detergent sit in the door gasket folds after every load, growing mold and a musty smell that gets into clean clothes
- **Toothbrush holders** (bathroom, opp. 26): Water and toothpaste pool in the closed bottom of cup-style holders, turning into slime that is hard to reach and clean
- **Trigger spray bottles** (cleaning, opp. 26): Nozzles clog with dried cleaner, seals wear so the trigger sputters, and the last inch can't be sprayed because the tube no longer reaches

### Bad timing (2)

- **Washer & dryer chimes** (laundry, opp. 26): The end-of-cycle chime is very loud by default, is silenced with hidden button combinations that differ by model, and some beeps can't be turned off at all
- **Bedside clocks & chargers** (bedroom, opp. 19): Clock displays and charging lights are too bright for a dark room, and dimming offers only two or three still-bright steps, so people tape over them

### Mapping mismatch (2)

- **Shower valves & controls** (bathroom, opp. 38): Every shower works differently; people can't tell which way is hot, how to switch tub to shower, or where the control is
- **Stovetop controls** (kitchen, opp. 21): A row of knobs controls a 2×2 grid of burners, so people turn on the wrong burner
