Desk Blueprint

How the one-person agent desk is organized, what each layer is allowed to do, which strategies are at which stage, and what would prove the plan wrong. Every trade on this desk is paper; a person types EXECUTE before anything real.

01The rule of the house

Four layers, and the boundary between them is the design. Models judge and explain; code decides and limits; a person approves. A wrong model answer can cost a trade. It can never place one.

Layer 1 · Claude Opus 5.5

Head agent

Research, strategy specs, code, reviews, the daily brief. Talks to you on the Desk Head page and hands bounded work to Claude Code sessions.

Never sizes, approves or places an order.

Layer 2 · Routines + sessions

Orchestration

The 16:40 ET close run, the Kalshi and carry refreshes, pull-request CI. The prompt calls AgenKit this layer; agenkit.xyz is a coding kit, useful for building, not for running the desk.

Never disarms the kill switch or edits ledgers.

Layer 3 · TypeSafe JEV

Typed judge

Answers narrow yes/no, choice and score questions over a structured state in roughly 0.1–0.5 s. Every answer is stored and scored against outcomes.

Veto only: never triggers, sizes up, or blocks an exit.

Layer 4 · Code + you

Policy and approval

Signals, sizing, limits, the kill switch, receipts. Real orders need a preview under 5 minutes old and your EXECUTE.

The final authority. Nothing overrides it.

06Risk engine and kill switch

Real paper book
5% per position, 150% gross, 20% per group, 1% of ADV; halts at −3% day or −10% drawdown; 15-minute macro blackout; quotes ≤120 s old; preview → EXECUTE within 5 minutes.
Put book
40% of NAV secured per position, 100% total; spreads under 10% of mid; exits at 50% credit, 3× credit, 15 sessions, 7 DTE, the session before earnings.
Spread book
Max loss ≤20% of NAV, one open, 1–2 wide, ≥20% credit/width; exits at 50% / 2× / 15 sessions / 7 DTE / earnings.
Trend book
Long or flat, no leverage, sleeves 50/30/20, 20% trailing stop per asset, 20% book drawdown kill.
Kill switch
One switch, ledger/kill.json. Arms itself on a receipt breach, a put book under $90k, a spread book under $350, or a trend book 20% off peak. Entries stop; exits run. Only you disarm it.

07Execution records

Every paper fill carries strategy_id, decision_id, model_version, expected_px (the mid at decision), fill_px, entry and exit slippage, and the JEV answers. Summaries report round-trip slippage as a share of credit. When the desk goes live, the same record is written before and after every real order, so paper and live can be compared line by line.

10Five crypto strategies

From the desk's research on 29 September 2026 (docs/research/crypto-strategies.md). Edge figures are estimates unless marked sourced. The hurdle for every one of them is the 3-month T-bill at 4.24%.

1 · Trend, vol-targeted, long or flat via ETFs

S-TREND · trend-etf-v1 · paper now
Thesis
Crypto moves in trends driven by leveraged retail attention; slow signals avoid noise. The main value is cutting the left tail (flat through June 2026's drop from $73k to $58k).
Rules
SMA20 over SMA100 on 2 closes; off on the reverse or a 20% trailing stop. Weight = sleeve × min(1, 30% / RV30). Trade IBIT, ETHA, BSOL.
Today
BTC, ETH and SOL all long; about 65% invested at a 30% vol target.
Edge
Estimated −3% to +8%/yr over T-bills; cost drag ≈0.2%/yr through ETFs (6–11%/yr through Robinhood crypto spot).
$500 · $10k
Survives costs; edge ≈$0–40/yr · ≈$0–800/yr with a $2k drawdown kill.
Bear case
Chop like July–August 2026 whipsaws; weekend gaps hit while ETFs are closed; this desk's own momentum backtest failed.
Invalidation
Walk-forward net Sharpe under 0.3, or paper tracking error over 1%/month.

2 · US-legal funding carry

S-CARRY-PERP · data first
Thesis
Leveraged longs pay shorts; hold IBIT or ETHA and short Coinbase's perp-style nano futures to collect funding with no price exposure.
Rules
Enter when 7-day funding ≥ T-bill + 3 and 30-day ≥ T-bill + 2; exit when 7-day falls under the T-bill, funding is negative 3 days, a margin alert fires or the hedge drifts over 3%.
Edge
Sourced: carry averaged ≈7% (2019–2024) and fell after ETFs. Coinbase funding is premium-only and likely lower; one snapshot shows ≈2.6%.
$500 · $10k
Not viable (≈$12/yr vs $21 of T-bills) · break-even at 7%, +$256/yr at 12%.
Bear case
High carry predicts crashes; margin calls on the short leg while the ETF gain sits at another broker. Hyperliquid is off-limits to US persons.

3 · Dated basis cash-and-carry

S-BASIS-DATED · alert only
Rule
Basis B = (F/S − 1) × 365 / DTE. Enter when B − costs − T-bill ≥ 3.5 points and DTE is 30–120; hold to expiry.
Now
CME ≈5.3%, below the ≈7.8% trigger; 2026 basis has been under the bill on 64% of days. Same bet as carry (rank correlation +0.75).

4 · Token-unlock shorts

S-UNLOCK · spec
Rule
Short Coinbase alt perp-style futures 7 days before a team or investor cliff unlock of ≥2% of supply; out at T+4, a +8% stop vs BTC, or −10% take-profit.
Evidence
Keyrock: 90% of unlocks pressure price; unlocks.app found +20% at 15 days for investor cliffs. Market makers pre-hedge. Contract sizes unverified. Testable only as a small $10k sleeve.

5 · Funding-extreme overlay

S-FUNDX · risk gate
Rule
When BTC funding sits in the top decile of 180 days for 24 hours, halve the trend book's BTC weight for 72 hours and halve carry capital.
Evidence
Top-decile funding → −1.18% median 72 h (n = 37); the negative-funding signal is not robust. Too weak to trade alone, useful as a gate.

Rejected: cross-sectional momentum (20–50%/yr cost drag at retail fees), stablecoin yield (3.5–4.7%, at or under T-bills with more risk), weekend effects (no current source), ETF-flow chasing (flows lag the basis).

12WHAT COULD I BE WRONG ABOUT?

  • The edge may not be real. This desk's own stock-signal backtest found nothing. Published crypto trend Sharpes above 1.5 come from bull-heavy samples. Carry, basis and unlock drift are all being arbitraged by larger, faster capital. The T-bill at 4.24% is the default winner and every book has to beat it on the same capital.
  • The catalyst may be priced. Unlock shorts at T−7 may be late (market makers hedge weeks out). High funding is a crash warning as much as an income source.
  • Costs and slippage. Spread fills at the natural price, crossed quotes (Robinhood showed one today), ETF expense ratios and futures commissions are partly estimates. Slippage is now recorded per trade so this can be measured instead of assumed.
  • JEV can be confidently wrong. It sees only the packet, and headlines are attacker-controllable text. That is why it can only veto, never touches exits or sizing, gets cleaned structured input, and is dropped automatically if its vetoes don't pay under the pre-registered test. Confidence is not a win rate.
  • Liquidity can vanish. Option spreads widen in stress, ETFs can't trade on weekends while crypto moves, and single-name puts gap through strikes. Exits run even when the kill switch is armed, but a gap still fills where it fills.
  • The regime can change. 2026 already has basis under the bill most days and months of negative funding. If crypto volatility converges toward equities, trend works less; if a crisis hits, the put books lose together.
  • Exchanges and data fail. A connector outage means no data, and the routine then writes a NO DATA receipt instead of trading on stale numbers. Offshore venues and their data are legal gray zones for a US person, so they are data only.
  • Strategies are correlated. Short puts, put spreads, crypto trend and carry are all, underneath, bets that markets don't crash. Five books may be about two independent bets.
  • Size. At $500 no active stream beats paying down card debt (≈22%) or T-bills in dollars. The paper run exists to prove the process, not to produce income.

Priorities, in order: survival (kill switch, human approval, no leverage), reproducibility (receipts, decision IDs, fixed bars), then risk-adjusted return.
